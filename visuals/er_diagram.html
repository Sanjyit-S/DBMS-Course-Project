Teacher Viva Defense: The 5 Critical Questions for this Diagram
Q1: Why is 'Character' shown with a double rectangle, and what is its Primary Key?
"Ma'am, Character is a Weak Entity (existence-dependent on Account via the identifying relationship Has). It does not have a globally unique key of its own; CharName has a dashed underline, meaning it is only a Partial Key (Discriminator). In the relational schema, its Primary Key is the composite key {AcctName, CharName}."
Q2: What does the dashed oval around 'PlayersIn' represent?
"The dashed oval indicates a Derived Attribute. PlayersIn is not physically stored in the database table to avoid redundancy; it is calculated at runtime by executing an aggregation query: SELECT COUNT(*) FROM Character WHERE RegionName = Region.RegionName."
Q3: What does the double oval around 'Foliage' represent, and how do you normalize it?
"The double oval signifies a Multivalued Attribute—a single region can have multiple foliage types (e.g. Pine trees, ferns, moss). Storing multiple values in one cell violates First Normal Form (1NF). To normalize it to 1NF, we decompose it into a separate child table: Region_Foliage(RegionName, Foliage) with composite primary key {RegionName, Foliage}."
Q4: What are the purple ovals 'LastPlayed', 'CreatedOn', and 'WhenCreated'?
"Those are Descriptive Attributes of a Relationship. They belong neither solely to the parent entity nor the child entity, but to the association event itself (e.g., when the account created or last played that specific character, or when an item template was instantiated)."
Q5: Identify the Many-to-Many (M:N) relationship and explain how to implement it in SQL.
"The M:N relationship is RanInto between Character (m) and Creep (n). Because relational tables cannot directly store M:N associations without violating 1NF, we create a junction table Character_RanInto_Creep containing Foreign Keys to both entities, with composite Primary Key {(AcctName, CharName), CreepName}."
